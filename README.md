# Video claim-research: taking a video apart claim by claim

A method, and the system around it, for checking what a video claims: split the transcript into claims and research each one against primary sources, official records, and the creator's earlier statements before ruling on each.

**My role:** I commissioned the verification and set the standard for a verdict. Research agents gather and cite; the run and the synthesis were executed by my agent Atlas. A claim is ruled "supported" only when the ruling names the source that settles it; otherwise the ruling says nothing does.

## The worked example
A popular video claimed that aircraft Doppler audio in a recording could place it within about 100 meters of a specific spot. Checked claim by claim:
- **The physics: supported, not new.** Single-microphone Doppler estimation of an aircraft's altitude and speed is published work: Ferguson (1992, J. Acoust. Soc. Am.) recovered both from one ground microphone. The step with no published prior demonstration, so far as this research found, is locating an unknown recording against archived flight tracks. The physics is real; the application appears to be the video's contribution.
- **The headline number: unverified.** The ~100 m figure comes only from the creator, no independent replication exists, and the ruling says so instead of rounding it to "debunked" or "confirmed."
- **The weakest point: the recording chain.** Whether the effect survives the codec the recording passed through, and the noise suppression applied to it, is the claim with the least evidence. The creator's in-video test is the only evidence.

Nine claims were graded, each with a verdict and its sources. Verdicts use a five-step scale: supported, partially supported, mixed, weakest-unverified, unverified. The ledger has an explicit could-not-verify section.

A video can be right about the physics and unverifiable on its headline; the rulings stay separate.
