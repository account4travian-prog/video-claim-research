## Summary

**The physics and every building block of the method are real, textbook/peer-reviewed, and in several cases decades old. The specific demonstration — locating an unknown recording to ~100 m, continent-wide, against ADS-B archives — is, as of 3 Nov 2026, supported only by the creator's own video and its (unusually detailed) description: no code or data were released, and no independent replication of the aircraft method or expert review had appeared within the first day.** Overall the claims are mostly *supported* at the level of principle, *partially supported* where the video generalises ("essentially all flights", codec survival), and the headline ~100 m result itself is *unverifiable* externally so far.

The video's own description (primary source for what was actually done) states: ADS-B position/altitude/velocity filtered in Python by ICAO type-code for prop vs jet; a **200 m candidate grid**; CPA and sound-propagation delay from 3D slant distance using a DEM; candidates filtered by the **920-second offset between two flyovers** plus a silence constraint; the exact Doppler curve modelled **using emission-time radial velocity from interpolated ADS-B data**; a locked two-event scoring method; and a repeat run on a **single 1-minute clip of known time, US-wide, which "successfully scored the true location 1st."** It also states plainly: **"No code or tools are being released."**

## Findings

### Claim 1 — Propeller tonal signature ~100–130 Hz, often inaudible, spectrogram-visible, penetrates buildings
**Verdict: SUPPORTED** (with the caveat that the exact fundamental varies by type/RPM).

- Propeller noise "predominantly consists of a fundamental tone at the [blade passing frequency], plus a series of diminishing-amplitude harmonics… the BPF component typically has a very high source-level amplitude and a relatively low frequency, **typically around 100 Hz**," and at cruise altitudes the Doppler-shifted BPF tone and low-order harmonics are the principal component heard on the ground; the atmosphere acts as a low-pass filter favouring these low frequencies. — InterNoise 2014, "Adapting a propeller noise model for aircraft at cruising altitudes" — http://www.acoustics.asn.au/conference_proceedings/INTERNOISE2014/papers/p791.pdf
- Independently: "the frequency of the fundamental, **typically about 100 Hz**, is determined by the rotation rate and the number of propeller blades," and the first ~10 harmonics of a light aircraft were detectable in air, in the water column, and on sensors buried ~1 m in the seabed. — Buckingham & Richardson, "A light aircraft as a low-frequency sound source for acoustical oceanography," Gayana/SciELO — http://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0717-65382004000200013
- "Often inaudible" is physiologically plausible for a distant/attenuated flyover: the ISO 226 hearing threshold at 100 Hz is ~26.5 dB SPL (vs ~0 dB at 1–4 kHz), and equal-loudness data show a 100 Hz tone needs roughly 20 dB more SPL than a 1 kHz tone to sound equally loud — so a tone can be well above a microphone/spectrogram noise floor while below or near conscious perception, especially masked by indoor ambient sound. — ISO 226 table via https://blog.ericmarty.com/perceptually-uniform-pitch-loudness-scales-for-data-sonification/ ; AES, "Equal loudness contours" — https://aes.org/resources/audio-topics/loudness-project/learn-more/
- Low frequencies penetrating buildings is standard acoustics: low-frequency noise "travel[s] over long distances and easily penetrate[s] buildings," bending around obstacles and passing structural gaps where higher frequencies are absorbed by walls. — "How to Soundproof Against Low Frequency Noise" — https://lcfilter.com/index.php/2024/07/31/how-to-soundproof-against-low-frequency-noise/ (trade source; the underlying point — poor low-frequency transmission loss of ordinary walls — is uncontroversial)

### Claim 2 — Doppler curve shape encodes speed (total drop) and CPA slant distance (transition rate); deviations encode turns/accel/RPM changes
**Verdict: SUPPORTED — this is established, published method, not a new discovery.**

- Ferguson (1992) estimated a propeller aircraft's **altitude and speed from a single ground microphone** by fitting a Doppler model to the observed blade-rate variation, validated against the aircraft's own records at 250–1,500 ft and 150–250 kt. — Ferguson, "A ground-based narrow-band passive acoustic technique for estimating the altitude and speed of a propeller-driven aircraft," J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 92(3):1403–1407 — abstract at https://www.mendeley.com/catalogue/2cd19f7e-cbeb-3f50-a337-e7046f815359/
- A four-parameter model (speed, source frequency, CPA time, **CPA slant range**) fitted to instantaneous frequency is the standard formulation. — Saravanakumar et al., "Localization of low flying aircraft using acoustic vector sensor" — https://www.joast.org/index.php/joast/article/download/434/411/825
- A US patent on acoustic airport surveillance describes the same processing: "By examining the shape of the time-frequency curve through closest point of approach, Doppler [CPA] processing provides estimates of **time at the closest point of approach, as well as slant range and speed**." — US Patent 8,059,489 — http://patents.justia.com/patent/8059489
- The most direct modern corroboration: Seppi, Tape & Fee (2025) give an explicit estimation recipe — measure approaching frequency Fa and receding frequency Fr from the spectrogram (their difference/central frequency yields speed for a known sound speed), then use the **slope of the curve at CPA to calculate d₀, the slant distance at closest approach**. — Supplement to "Classification of Aircraft Types Using Seismic Data in Alaska," The Seismic Record — https://gsw.silverchair-cdn.com/gsw/Content_public/Journal/tsr/5/4/10.1785_0320250035/2/tsr-2025035_supplement.pdf?Expires=1788722242&Signature=pxym4Hd8KL3s3ZnXtws3MiGbPMRnFDcwbOY1BRcXh7ZblGbuqZl7PPskZR3vDONXf~Q1WcE5nmGgllPFVBY6uxM8rEymLaElh8b81mPjPdlP6NS9RUylNhOivRb609SgofZ7saxmlOSVWj~ggsgqhjRru8pMFMkG6hHr2ExiabXjKZvQKh797dkak1KforVLtzF~Re6VCB-oafq-DGuzHNDtGh7xlDVaj6hdZYnvCLCgPHh11qmLd0Hfadsyhr-oHislriiYa7HYf6~aB2jLSsHYpPQTM73ZpVYHC5qJr9xbVNdkjaSoEeLmNEKGjedADGo69bVCyNJ9fQbb5i5bBQ__&Key-Pair-Id=APKAIE5G5CRDK6RD3PGA
- Deviations-from-ideal being informative is also demonstrated: Seppi et al. "demonstrate how modes of flight, such as a propeller's rotational rate, alter these signatures" — they could detect RPM differences between a climbing vs descending flightseeing aircraft. Any change in radial-velocity history (turn, accel, RPM change) necessarily changes the received frequency history, since received frequency is a direct function of radial velocity at emission (see Claim 3). — Seppi et al. abstract — https://pubs.geoscienceworld.org/ssa/tsr/article/5/4/330/688104/Classification-of-Aircraft-Types-Using-Seismic

### Claim 3 — Emission-time (retarded-time) radial velocity must be used; reception-time velocity gives a materially different curve, not just a shifted one
**Verdict: SUPPORTED — standard aeroacoustics.**

- Retarded time is a foundational concept for moving sources: "the sound received at the observer time was not emitted from the source positions at [reception] time, but from their earlier positions at the retarded time," because the source moves during propagation. — Embry-Riddle, "Aeroacoustics of Flight Vehicles" textbook chapter — https://eaglepubs.erau.edu/introductiontoaerospaceflightvehicles/chapter/noise-of-flight-vehicles/
- The formal literature treats the emission↔reception time mapping as a non-trivial, solved-for relation (t_ret = t − |x − y(t_ret)|/c), and the (1 − M_r) factor linking the two time scales *is* the Doppler factor. — Casalino, "An advanced time approach for acoustic analogy predictions," J. Sound & Vibration 261 (2003) — https://acoustique.ec-lyon.fr/publi/casalino_jsv03a.pdf
- Seppi et al.'s forward model makes the point concretely: their Eq. (2) maps reception time t′ to emission-path time t via a term containing d₀/c and v²/c² corrections — i.e., the observed curve F(t′) is a *warped*, not merely translated, version of the emission-time curve. Because slant range changes by kilometres across a flyover, propagation delay changes by seconds across the event (at ~50–70 m/s the aircraft moves hundreds of metres during the delay), so using reception-time positions distorts the transition region asymmetrically. — Seppi supplement, URL as in Claim 2.
- The video's description confirms the creator did exactly this ("emission-time radial velocity from interpolated ADS-B data") — consistent with correct practice, and his emphasis on it is justified.

### Claim 4 — Historical ADS-B: essentially all CONUS flights, purchasable, prop/jet mappable by type code
**Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — the data and the type-code mapping are real; "essentially all flights" is overstated, and purchase is more restricted than the phrase suggests.**

Supported parts:
- ADS-B broadcasts position, altitude, speed and identity roughly every second (ADS-B Exchange archives global snapshots every 5 s since March 2020, and per-aircraft trace and 2 Hz "hires" trace files), and historical data is a commercial product. — ADS-B Exchange, "Sample Data" — https://www.adsbexchange.com/data-products/sample-data/
- Prop vs jet mapping is straightforward: ICAO Doc 8643 gives every type designator a 3-character description whose third character is engine type — **P = piston, T = turboprop/turboshaft, J = jet** (e.g., C172 = L1P, DH8D = L2T, A320 = L2J). The video description says it used "ICAO type-code classification for propeller vs jet types." — VATSIM Germany Knowledgebase, "ICAO Type Description" — https://knowledgebase.vatsim-germany.org/books/aircraft-knowledge/page/icao-type-description ; EUROCONTROL trrrj aircraft-type table — https://trrrj.ansperformance.eu/reference/aircrafttype.html

Limits / corrections:
- **Purchase friction (current terms):** ADS-B Exchange now sells data only as "ongoing subscription services with minimum annual commitments… **Historical backfills for up to 10 years are available to subscription customers only. ADS-B Exchange does not offer one-time data extracts or project-based access.**" So "purchasable" ≠ "buy one day cheaply" from that vendor today. Free first-of-the-month sample days exist, and free full-history alternatives exist (adsb.lol, below). — ADS-B Exchange, "Data Products" — https://www.adsbexchange.com/data-products/
- **Mandate scope:** Under 14 CFR 91.225 ADS-B Out is required only in Class A, B and C airspace, the 30 NM Mode C veil, and Class E at/above 10,000 ft MSL (CONUS). Below 10,000 ft outside B/C/veil airspace it is **not** required, and aircraft without an engine-driven electrical system are exempt — which matters because the small piston GA aircraft that produce the loudest low flyovers are precisely the segment most likely to be unequipped when operating outside rule airspace. — 14 CFR 91.225 summaries citing eCFR — https://legalclarity.org/where-is-ads-b-out-required-airspace-rules-explained/
- **Coverage gaps:** crowdsourced receiver networks have line-of-sight, terrain and density limits; low-altitude aircraft in rural/mountainous areas "may pass through 'blind spots'… even if the aircraft is transmitting," appearing intermittently or not at all — a plane can be clearly audible overhead and absent from trackers. — AvGeek Philippines — https://avgeek.ph/why-you-sometimes-hear-aircraft-overhead-but-dont-see-them-on-flight-tracking-websites/ ; OpenSky Report 2025 (arXiv) on crowdsourced ADS-B gaps — https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06254v1
- **Blocked aircraft nuance (cuts both ways):** FAA LADD/PIA only scrub the FAA SWIM feed and participating commercial trackers; per the FAA, LADD "does not impact the ADS-B broadcast data," so unfiltered crowdsourced networks (ADS-B Exchange's model) still capture those aircraft's position/velocity — good for this method. It does not help for aircraft with no transponder/ADS-B at all, or military traffic flying with transponders off. — UAS News US — https://uasnewsus.com/articles/faa-safety-mandate-weather-tax-tool-la-county-adsb-aircraft-discovery-program ; OPS Group — https://ops.group/blog/are-you-ads-being-watched/?replytocom=108776

### Claim 7 — Prior art: is this novel?
**Verdict: The components are 30+ years old in the literature; the specific inversion — locating an *unknown consumer recording* by matching its Doppler curves against archived flight tracks over a continent — appears to be a novel *public demonstration*, not a novel physical method. No published prior demo of that exact inversion was found.**

Prior art, by component:
- **Single-microphone Doppler estimation of an aircraft's speed/altitude/CPA:** Ferguson 1992 (above); Ferguson & Quinn on UAV spectrogram contours; closed-form solutions (Timlelt et al., Digital Signal Processing 2017 — estimates "speed, altitude, source frequency and CPA time" for prop aircraft/helicopters from one ground microphone); microphone-array trajectory estimation (Tong et al., JASA 2016). — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1051200416302238 ; https://www.bohrium.com/en/paper-details/estimation-of-low-altitude-moving-target-trajectory-using-single-acoustic-array/814535259763769346-582
- **Doppler comb + flight-tracker ground truth (closest analogue):** Seppi, Tape & Fee, *The Seismic Record*, 18 Nov 2025 — 303 seismometers, **1,216 known flights / 48 types**, flight parameters (closest time, closest distance, speed, source frequencies) estimated from Doppler curves and checked against **Flightradar24** ground truth; piston/turboprop/jet/helicopter distinguishable. Difference: their sensors' locations were known and the goal was identifying the *aircraft*; colsto inverts it to find the *recorder*. Published ~1 year before the video. — https://pubs.geoscienceworld.org/ssa/tsr/article/5/4/330/688104/Classification-of-Aircraft-Types-Using-Seismic ; press release — https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1106534
- **Using a Doppler comb scientifically:** Buckingham et al. (2002–2004) used propeller Doppler combs to measure sediment sound speed — the comb-as-measurement idea is old.
- **ENF (the closest forensic cousin):** Electric Network Frequency analysis timestamps/authenticates recordings from mains hum (used by the Metropolitan Police Forensic Audio Lab), and Garg, Hajj-Ahmad & Wu (ICASSP 2013) explicitly proposed **geo-location estimation from ENF signals** — proof that "inaudible background signature in a recording leaks time/place" is an established forensic research programme. — https://terpconnect.umd.edu/~minwu/public_paper/Conf13/1305_ENFLocalize_icassp13.pdf ; Robert Heaton, "How to date a recording using background electrical noise" — https://robertheaton.com/enf/?ref=the-browser

### Claim 8 — Defense claims
**(a) High-pass filtering does not reliably remove the signature — PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (directionally right, overstated if read as "filtering can't work").**
- The prop fundamental (~100–130 Hz) sits *inside* the voice pitch range — adult male speech F0 is 85–180 Hz, female 165–255 Hz — so any high-pass gentle enough to keep natural-sounding voice (cutoff ≲100 Hz, typical 6–12 dB/oct "rumble filters") leaves the fundamental largely intact. — Voice frequency summary — https://alchetron.com/Voice-frequency
- But the harmonics are the loophole in the *other* direction too: BPF harmonics at 2×, 3×, 4×… (200–520+ Hz) are also Doppler-shifted copies of the same curve, and the published methods work from *any* clean harmonic (Seppi et al. deliberately pick "the highest-amplitude, clearest harmonic"; Ferguson's method needs only the blade-rate line). Even a telephone-style 300 Hz high-pass — which would kill the fundamental and damage voice naturalness — leaves the 3rd+ harmonics carrying the full Doppler shape. Removing *all* harmonics means filtering away most of the speech band itself.
- Where the claim overreaches: a steep, deliberately-chosen filter chain plus the fact that harmonics diminish in amplitude (and suffer more atmospheric absorption over distance) would in practice defeat *distant, faint* flyovers. "Not reliable as a casual fix" is supported; "cannot be filtered" would not be.

**(b) Loud foreground speech does not prevent extraction — SUPPORTED in principle.**
- Extraction is in the frequency domain: a persistent narrowband tone occupies specific STFT bins for tens of seconds while speech is broadband and time-varying, and log-scale spectrograms compress dynamic range so weak narrowband components remain visible alongside loud ones. This is the same reason ENF analysts routinely extract a faint 50/60 Hz hum from under speech, and why Seppi et al. could pull clean Doppler curves from noisy seismic records. Limits the video doesn't stress: clipping/saturation, aggressive AGC pumping, or a foreground that is itself a loud sustained tone in the same bins would break it. — ENF practice — https://robertheaton.com/enf/?ref=the-browser ; log-spectrogram dynamic range — https://www.flyriver.com/g/logarithmic-spectrogram

**(c) Survival through phone-call compression (Opus/EVS) and noise suppression — PARTIALLY SUPPORTED for codecs; WEAK/UNVERIFIABLE for noise suppression. This is the video's softest claim.**
- Codecs alone: plausible. EVS's only built-in high-pass is at **~20 Hz**, it supports fullband (20 Hz–20 kHz) and is designed for generic audio/music as well as speech; Opus fullband likewise codes from near-DC to 20 kHz. Neither codec inherently strips a 100–130 Hz tone and its harmonics. — Nokia, "The 3GPP Enhanced Voice Services (EVS) codec" white paper — https://www.nokia.com/asset/f/200002/ ; Xiph, "Opus Recommended Settings" (fullband listed as 3–20,000 Hz) — https://wiki.xiph.org/index.php?title=Opus_Recommended_Settings&oldid=16497
- Noise suppression: cuts the other way. Classical spectral subtraction and RNNoise-style suppressors are *specifically designed* to remove steady non-speech tones — documentation for RNNoise-based tools says steady noise (fan, AC, mains hum) "goes almost completely." A Doppler tone is, however, *non-stationary* (it sweeps ~±15–20% in frequency over the pass), which stationary-noise estimators track poorly, and suppression is app/device-dependent and often absent or mild. — RNNoise tool documentation — https://stayput.dev/remove-background-noise-from-audio ; https://audiosaw.com/noise-reduction
- No published experiment was found that passes a real prop-flyover recording through Opus/EVS + production noise suppression and measures Doppler-curve survival. The creator's in-video test is currently the only evidence — treat as **unverified**.

### Claim 9 — Feasibility, cost, compute, failure modes
**Verdict: FEASIBLE for a skilled individual at hobbyist cost — mainly because free ADS-B history now exists — provided the inputs cooperate. The "~50 GB of parquet for one day" figure in the brief is plausible for high-resolution/commercial data but is not a floor: a usable global day is ~2 GB compressed from adsb.lol.**

- **Data scale/cost:** adsb.lol publishes a full global day free (ODbL): daily tarballs of **1.6–1.8 GB**, converting to ~45 parquet files of ~1M records each (~45M records/day). ADS-B Exchange's fuller feed is far larger (ClickHouse's adsb.exposed notes ~1.2 billion records/day for ADS-B Exchange vs 30–50M/day for adsb.lol), so a ~50 GB US/full-resolution parquet working set is a realistic order of magnitude for that source — but ADS-B Exchange currently requires an annual subscription (no one-off extracts), while the free route needs only a few GB download. A hobbyist write-up demonstrates day-scale DuckDB/parquet analysis of this data on a home machine with an 8 TB disk. — Mark Litwintschik, "Global Flight Tracking" / "Aircraft Route Analysis" — https://tech.marksblogg.com/global-flight-tracking-adsb.html , http://tech.marksblogg.com/aircraft-route-analysis-adsb.html ; ClickHouse adsb.exposed — https://github.com/ClickHouse/adsb.exposed ; ADS-B Exchange terms — https://www.adsbexchange.com/data-products/
- **Compute:** the forward model is analytic and cheap (Seppi et al. fit 1,193 crossings with a 5-parameter model); the creator's own recipe — pre-filter candidates by velocity/CPA from the measured curve, then by the inter-flyover time offset, then forward-score survivors on a 200 m grid — reduces a continental grid search to a tractable filter-and-score pipeline in Python. No exotic hardware is implied by any source.
- **Required inputs (per the video's own description):** the recording's **date** (the second demo additionally had **known time of day** and only needed 1 minute / one flyover); at least one — in the main demo two, 920 s apart — sufficiently close prop flyovers during the clip; audio that retains the ~100 Hz region.
- **Known/likely failure modes:**
  1. *The aircraft isn't in the data* — non-equipped GA outside rule airspace, transponder-off traffic, low-altitude/rural receiver gaps (Claim 4). This is the biggest practical hole: it silently removes candidate flyovers.
  2. *Sound-speed/model mismatch* — effective sound speed varies with temperature/wind profile (Seppi et al. used a modelled c = 320 m/s, not 343); refraction can create acoustic shadow zones where a geometrically-close flyover is inaudible, and turbulence smears spectral lines. — Seppi supplement (above); Wu & Redonnet on refraction/shadow zones — https://repository.hkust.edu.hk/ir/bitstream/1783.1-132634/1/132634.pdf
  3. *Recording chain* — the common worry about phone mics is partly misplaced: a peer-reviewed smartphone study found tested phones had *emphasis* at 85–140 Hz and roll-off only below ~30 Hz, i.e., a 100 Hz fundamental survives typical phone hardware well. The real risks are app-level processing: voice-recorder high-pass/noise suppression, AGC, wind noise (broadband, concentrated at low frequencies, directly masking the band of interest), and platform re-encoding. YouTube's Opus (~128 kbps, fullband) should preserve the tone reasonably; aggressive short-form pipelines are untested. — Awan et al., J. Voice — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10545813/
  4. *Ambiguity* — a single flyover constrains the recorder to a locus of positions consistent with one (velocity, CPA-distance, CPA-time) triple; the video's precision came from *two* events with a fixed 920 s offset plus a silence constraint, or (demo 2) from known time. One faint flyover + unknown time of day is a much weaker problem than the headline suggests.
  5. *RPM instability* — prop RPM (hence fundamental frequency) changes with climb/descent/power, which both adds signal (Claim 2) and breaks naive constant-f₀ curve extraction.
